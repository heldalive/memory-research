# Revoked but Still Authoritative: An Empirical Study of Revocation Enforcement in Agent-Memory Systems

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08258v1)

abstract only. Checked 2026-09-17T18:48:23.461Z.

The paper [S1] investigates membership inference attacks on chat agent memory, proposing MRMMIA as a method to detect whether a memory unit belongs to an agent's store through multi-recall probes under black-box, gray-box, and white-box settings. It demonstrates that MRMMIA outperforms baselines in detecting membership signals, highlighting privacy risks in memory retention. [S2] evaluates revocation enforcement in agent-memory systems, showing that revoked facts are often still retrieved and acted upon, despite being marked invalid, and finds no system enforces revocation by default across policy scenarios. Neither study reports a controlled experiment with token-constrained memory under measurable state evolution and explicit baseline comparisons as required by the objective.
